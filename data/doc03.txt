Title : Understanding Credit Risk

Abstarct : 
Definition : Credit risk is the potential for financial loss to a lender when a borrower fails to repay a loan or meet contractual obligations.
Credit risk is the chance that a borrower may default on a loan, leading to potential financial loss for the lender. It is central in finance as it affects lending decisions and interest rates.

Lenders use tools like credit scores and the five Cs of credit—capacity, capital, conditions, character, and collateral—to evaluate borrowers’ creditworthiness and manage credit risk effectively.

Credit ratings by agencies like Moody’s and Fitch play a crucial role in assessing the risk associated with corporate and municipal bonds, influencing investment decisions.

High credit risk often results in higher interest rates as lenders seek compensation for the increased risk of default. Borrowers with high creditworthiness, by contrast, benefit from lower rates.

Banks manage credit risk by setting strict lending criteria, continuously monitoring credit portfolios, and adjusting to changes in a borrower’s credit profile, thus reducing potential losses.
